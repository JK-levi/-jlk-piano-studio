🎹 Week 9 — Late-Night R&B / Neo-Soul
Warm electric piano, beautiful chords, and a slow, effortless groove
25 minutes | 80 BPM | Roland GO:KEYS 3

This week, we're moving away from last week's punchy funk and into something softer, moodier, and more intimate.

Think Sade, Lauryn Hill, and D'Angelo.

Today's goal: Learn four beautiful chord shapes, discover how smoothly they connect, and play them over a relaxed R&B drumbeat.

We're also introducing a new skill: keeping some right-hand fingers in place while other notes change. This makes your playing sound smoother without requiring more complicated chords.

① Warm-up — 3 minutes
Start around the middle of your keyboard.

We're using five notes:

D♭ – E♭ – F – A♭ – B♭

Four are black keys. F is white.

Play slowly
LH: — | RH: D♭ → E♭ → F → A♭ → B♭

Come back down:

LH: — | RH: B♭ → A♭ → F → E♭ → D♭

Now make two little musical phrases.

LH: — | RH: F → A♭ → B♭

Pause.

LH: — | RH: A♭ → F → E♭ → D♭

Repeat these a few times.

Don't worry about keeping a perfect beat yet.

Listen to how these notes sound together. They're going to become your little melody-making toolkit later.

② Four chords — 6 minutes
These are especially nice because the right-hand shapes move gradually rather than jumping around the keyboard.

Keep your left hand below middle C and your right hand around or above middle C.

① Warm and settled
LH: D♭ + A♭ | RH: F + A♭ + C

② Softer, slightly melancholy
LH: B♭ + F | RH: F + A♭ + D♭

③ A little more tension
LH: E♭ + B♭ | RH: G♭ + B♭ + D♭

④ Floating, wanting to return
LH: A♭ + E♭ | RH: G♭ + C + E♭

Your entire progression
① → ② → ③ → ④ → repeat

For now, play each chord once.

Count:

1 – 2 – 3 – 4

Press both hands on 1.

Hold everything through 2, 3, 4.

Release just before the next 1 and change chords.

Something to notice: When you move from chord ① to chord ②, your RH keeps F and A♭ exactly where they are. Only the top note moves from C to D♭.

That's the sound we're exploring this week: small movements creating beautiful changes.

③ Give it an R&B rhythm — 5 minutes
We're going to play the same four chords, but make them feel less like an exercise and more like an actual keyboard accompaniment.

Count out loud
1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &

Each number is a main beat. Each & is halfway between beats.

Count	1	&	2	&	3	&	4	&
LH	PRESS	HOLD	HOLD	HOLD	HOLD	HOLD	HOLD	RELEASE
RH	PRESS	HOLD	RELEASE	—	—	PRESS	HOLD	RELEASE
Exactly what to do
Start with chord ①.

LH: D♭ + A♭ | RH: F + A♭ + C

1: Press both hands together.

& after 1: Keep holding.

2: Release RH. Keep LH down.

& after 2: Nothing. Let the music breathe.

3: Keep holding LH.

& after 3: Press RH again.

4: Keep holding.

& after 4: Release everything.

On the next 1, move to chord ②.

That's it!

You're playing the right-hand chord twice, but the second hit arrives between the main beats.

This creates a gentle push forward without making the rhythm busy.

Practice this with all four shapes.

First 4 minutes — Find the groove
Play the same four-chord progression:

① LH: D♭ + A♭ | RH: F + A♭ + C
② LH: B♭ + F | RH: F + A♭ + D♭
③ LH: E♭ + B♭ | RH: G♭ + B♭ + D♭
④ LH: A♭ + E♭ | RH: G♭ + C + E♭

One full measure (eight counts of 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &) for each chord.

Keep the left hand steady and let the right hand gently fall into the spaces between the drum hits.

Don't worry if your timing isn't perfect.

Try to feel where the drumbeat wants your notes to land.

Next 4 minutes — Make something of your own
Let's simplify the left hand to one note per chord.

① LH: D♭ | RH: F + A♭ + C
② LH: B♭ | RH: F + A♭ + D♭
③ LH: E♭ | RH: G♭ + B♭ + D♭
④ LH: A♭ | RH: G♭ + C + E♭

Keep looping.

Now occasionally replace the RH chord with a few notes from your warm-up.

Use:

D♭ – E♭ – F – A♭ – B♭

Here's one little phrase:

LH: D♭ | RH: F → A♭ → B♭

Pause.

LH: B♭ | RH: A♭ → F → E♭

Then return to your chords.

You're not trying to play a complicated solo.

You're trying to find a short phrase that sounds like it belongs with your chords.

When you hear something you like, repeat it.

Let the drums keep going while you experiment. Restart the video as needed to fill your practice time.

⑤ Optional GO:KEYS 3 sound experiment — 3 minutes
This week, try Dual.

Dual means two instrument sounds play together whenever you press a key.

Your sounds
UPPER: Soft Stage EP
LOWER: Air Pad

These are both actual factory tones on your GO:KEYS 3. Soft Stage EP is in the E.Piano1 category, and Air Pad is in Synth Pad/Str. 

Set it up
Press [split/dual] until the screen displays Dual.
Select UPPER with [f2], then choose Soft Stage EP.
Select LOWER with [f1], then choose Air Pad.
If the pad is too prominent, select LOWER, open [f4] (SETTING), and reduce its Level. 
Now play:

LH: D♭ + A♭ | RH: F + A♭ + C

Hold the notes.

Listen for the soft electric-piano sound at the beginning and the airy synth sound behind it.

Play the whole progression again.

You haven't changed a single note, but the atmosphere should feel completely different.

🎧 Apple Music — This week's inspiration

Listen on Apple Music

Notice how the notes and chords seem to flow smoothly into one another.

Also listen to the bass: it moves independently, but never overwhelms the arrangement.

What to borrow: That beautiful, relaxed sense of movement.

2. Lauryn Hill & D'Angelo — Nothing Even Matters
Listen on Apple Music

This one is especially worth listening to with headphones.

The original recording includes Rhodes electric piano, and the keyboard textures are a big part of its warmth. 

Notice how the instruments leave room for the voices. The chords have character, but they don't demand attention constantly.

What to borrow: The intimacy of the sound and the willingness to leave silence.

These songs are references for the feel, not the exact chord progression you're practicing.

⭐ Your keyboard cheat sheet
If you forget everything else, keep this in front of you:

The four shapes
① LH: D♭ + A♭ | RH: F + A♭ + C

② LH: B♭ + F | RH: F + A♭ + D♭

③ LH: E♭ + B♭ | RH: G♭ + B♭ + D♭

④ LH: A♭ + E♭ | RH: G♭ + C + E♭

Your rhythm
Count: 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &
LH: Press on 1. Hold until the final &.
RH: Press on 1 and & after 3. Release on 2 and the final &.

Your goal this week
Make those four shapes feel so familiar that you can stop concentrating on finding the notes and start listening to the music you're making.

The new skill is smoothness.

You practiced short, punchy rhythms last week. This week, I want the notes to feel connected, relaxed, and almost effortless.

And if you find yourself enjoying a particular little melody over the drumbeat, forget the rest of the exercise for a while and explore it. That's where the fun begins.
