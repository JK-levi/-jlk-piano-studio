This week: Warm R&B / late-night groove
Time: about 25 minutes
Goal: get one smooth 4-chord loop into your hands, then stop “practicing” it and start making it your own.
1. Black-key warm-up — 4 minutes
Use only these right-hand notes:

D♭ – E♭ – G♭ – A♭ – B♭

Start with this little phrase:

RH: A♭ → B♭ → G♭ → E♭

Then answer it with:

RH: G♭ → E♭ → D♭

Play slowly. Leave space between the two phrases. After a minute or two, forget my pattern and just wander around those five notes.

Your only job: when you accidentally play something you like, repeat it.
2. Your main 4-chord loop — 7 minutes
Play these in order.

1. LH: D♭ + A♭ | RH: D♭ + F + A♭

2. LH: B♭ + F | RH: D♭ + F + A♭

3. LH: G♭ + D♭ | RH: D♭ + F + B♭

4. LH: A♭ + E♭ | RH: C + E♭ + A♭

Then go straight back to #1.

Notice something important: from chord 1 to chord 2, your right hand doesn't move at all. Only the left hand changes.

For the first few minutes, don't add rhythm. Press both hands together and hold each chord for four counts:

1 – 2 – 3 – 4

Change on the next 1.

Keep looping until you no longer have to hunt for every note.
3. Give it a groove — 5 minutes
Now count:

1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &

For every chord:

1: press BOTH hands
2: keep holding
3: lift and replay the RIGHT hand only
4: let everything ring

Think:

BOTH — hold — RH — hold

Then move to the next chord on the next 1.

Don't try to make it fancy yet. The slight right-hand hit on beat 3 is enough to turn the progression from “four chords” into something that starts feeling like music.
One optional variation
Once the basic rhythm feels automatic, move that second RH hit from 3 to the & after 2:

1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &
BOTH — — RH — — — —

That little early hit creates much more of an R&B pocket.
4. Put it over a beat — 7–8 minutes
Find a beat-only track around 76–84 BPM.

For the first couple minutes, simply play the four-chord loop exactly as above.

Then simplify the left hand to one note:

LH: D♭ | RH: D♭ + F + A♭
LH: B♭ | RH: D♭ + F + A♭
LH: G♭ | RH: D♭ + F + B♭
LH: A♭ | RH: C + E♭ + A♭

Once that feels secure, occasionally skip the RH chord and use your black-key notes instead:

D♭ – E♭ – G♭ – A♭ – B♭

Don't try to solo continuously. Play a few notes, then leave space while the beat continues.

That's where I want the lesson to turn into your music.
5. Optional GO:KEYS 3 sound experiment — 3 minutes
The GO:KEYS 3 supports both Split and Dual, so use Split this week: low keyboard for one sound, upper keyboard for another. Roland lists more than 1,100 ZEN-Core tones on the instrument. Roland

Keep it simple:

Left: choose a soft synth-bass tone.
Right: choose an electric-piano tone.

You don't need a pad this week—the bass + EP combination makes it much easier to hear exactly what each hand is contributing.

Keep the LH extremely simple:

D♭ → B♭ → G♭ → A♭

Let the electric piano handle the chords and little melodic answers above it.

What I want you to get out of this one
By the end, you should be able to turn on a beat and play:

D♭ → B♭ → G♭ → A♭

without needing to stop and calculate what comes next.

Once your hands know the loop, that's when the actual musical exploration starts.
