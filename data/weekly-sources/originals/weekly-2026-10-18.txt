🎹 Week 8 — Retro Funk & Soul
Think Stevie Wonder meets modern funky keyboard grooves.
Time: 25 minutes | Tempo: 95 BPM | Difficulty: Easy–Intermediate

This week we're going for something a little dirtier, funkier, and more playful.

Last week you practiced making your hands move independently with a relaxed Brazilian rhythm. Today we're keeping that independence but making everything short, punchy, and rhythmic.

The secret to funk is often what you don't play.

Today's goal: Get three chord shapes comfortable, learn one funky rhythm, and spend eight minutes making your own groove.

① Warm-up — 3 minutes
Five notes, a little attitude
Start around the middle of your keyboard.

LH: — | RH: C → E♭ → F → G → B♭

Come back down:

LH: — | RH: B♭ → G → F → E♭ → C

Now play this little phrase:

LH: — | RH: C → E♭ → F

Pause.

LH: — | RH: G → F → E♭ → C

Repeat it a few times.

Then start changing the rhythm. Make some notes short, others longer.

Don't worry about timing yet. Just get comfortable with those five notes.

② Your three chord shapes — 6 minutes
We're using three warm, slightly bluesy sounds.

Play them in this order:

①
LH: F + C | RH: A♭ + C + E♭

②
LH: B♭ + F | RH: A♭ + D + F

③
LH: C + G | RH: B♭ + E♭ + G

④ Back to shape ②
LH: B♭ + F | RH: A♭ + D + F

Then repeat from ①.

Your complete loop: ① → ② → ③ → ②

First, play them slowly.
Count:

1 – 2 – 3 – 4

Press both hands together on 1.

Hold everything through 2, 3, 4.

Release just before the next 1, then change chords.

Keep repeating until your fingers start remembering where to go.

Notice the darker sound of chord ① compared with the brighter chord ②.

③ Learn the funk rhythm — 5 minutes
This is today's main exercise.

We're going to make the left hand sound like a bass player and the right hand sound like a funky rhythm guitarist.

Count out loud:

1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &

Count	1	&	2	&	3	&	4	&
LH	●	—	—	—	●	—	—	—
RH	—	●	—	—	—	●	—	—
● = Press. A dash means don't press anything new.

Here's exactly what to do for chord ①:

LH: F + C | RH: A♭ + C + E♭

1: Press LH.
& after 1: Release LH and press RH.
2: Release RH.
& after 2: Silence.
3: Press LH again.
& after 3: Release LH and press RH.
4: Release RH.
& after 4: Silence.

Then change to chord ② on the next 1.

The silence is important!

Don't hold the notes. Each hit should be short.

Think:

BASS-chuck ... BASS-chuck ...

This is a form of syncopation, which simply means placing musical accents between the main numbered beats.

Practice the whole loop
① LH: F + C | RH: A♭ + C + E♭
② LH: B♭ + F | RH: A♭ + D + F
③ LH: C + G | RH: B♭ + E♭ + G
④ LH: B♭ + F | RH: A♭ + D + F

Use the exact same rhythm for each.

Once you can do that without stopping, you're ready for the fun part.

④ Play over a real funk drum groove — 8 minutes
I found a 95 BPM funk backing track with drums only.

Funk Drum Groove — 95 BPM, Drums Only (JunzBass Backing Tracks)
youtube.com
It's just the drums, so there's no bass or keyboard competing with what you're playing.

First 4 minutes: Lock in
Play your three shapes with the rhythm you just learned.

LH: F + C | RH: A♭ + C + E♭
LH: B♭ + F | RH: A♭ + D + F
LH: C + G | RH: B♭ + E♭ + G
LH: B♭ + F | RH: A♭ + D + F

Keep repeating.

Try to get your LH notes lined up with the drumbeat.

Your RH should feel like little rhythmic answers.

Don't speed up when something sounds exciting.

Listen to the drummer, not just yourself.

Next 4 minutes: Make something of your own
Keep the drum track going.

For the first couple of loops, simplify the left hand:

① LH: F | RH: A♭ + C + E♭
② LH: B♭ | RH: A♭ + D + F
③ LH: C | RH: B♭ + E♭ + G
④ LH: B♭ | RH: A♭ + D + F

Then occasionally stop playing the RH chords and use these notes to invent a melody:

C – E♭ – F – G – B♭

Try this simple phrase:

LH: F | RH: C → E♭ → F

Pause.

LH: B♭ | RH: G → F → E♭

Then return to playing the full chords.

Don't try to fill every moment.

If you find a little melody you like, repeat it several times.

That's exactly how a lot of great funk grooves develop: one short, memorable musical idea repeated with slight changes.

⑤ Optional GO:KEYS 3 experiment — 3 minutes
This week, use Split.

Split means assigning a different instrument sound to the lower and upper sections of your keyboard.

We're going for a classic funk combination.

LOWER: Fingered Bass
UPPER: Clav 1

Both are actual Roland GO:KEYS 3 factory tones. Fingered Bass is in the E.Bass category and Clav 1 is in the Clav category. 

A clavinet is an electric keyboard instrument with a short, bright, almost plucked sound. It's particularly associated with 1970s funk.

Set up Split
Press [split/dual] until the screen shows Split.
Select LOWER with [f1] and choose Fingered Bass.
Select UPPER with [f2] and choose Clav 1.
Those controls are documented in Roland's GO:KEYS 3 reference manual. 

Now play the simplified version:

① LH: F | RH: A♭ + C + E♭
② LH: B♭ | RH: A♭ + D + F
③ LH: C | RH: B♭ + E♭ + G
④ LH: B♭ | RH: A♭ + D + F

Keep LH below the keyboard's split point and RH above it.

Use the same rhythm as before.

The sound should immediately become punchier and more like two musicians playing together.

⭐ What to remember this week
Your entire practice revolves around just three shapes:

① LH: F + C | RH: A♭ + C + E♭
② LH: B♭ + F | RH: A♭ + D + F
③ LH: C + G | RH: B♭ + E♭ + G

Play them in this order: ① → ② → ③ → ②.

The new skill isn't another complicated chord. It's making your playing sound more intentional by shortening notes and leaving rhythmic space.

And when you get to the drum track, resist the urge to keep adding notes.

Find a groove you like. Stay with it. Enjoy how it feels.
