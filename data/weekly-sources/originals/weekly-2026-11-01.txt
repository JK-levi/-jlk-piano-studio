🎹 Week 10 — Cinematic Piano
25 minutes · 80 BPM · Roland GO:KEYS 3

This week we're going for something spacious and emotional, like a film soundtrack. Think Ludovico Einaudi or the quiet beginning of a dramatic movie scene.

Last week was about smooth R&B chords. This week we'll build on that by playing the right-hand notes one at a time, letting them rise over a sustained left hand.

Today's goal: Make four simple shapes sound like the beginning of your own soundtrack.

1. Warm up — 3 minutes
Start with a piano sound. Play these notes around the middle of your keyboard.

LH: — | RH: E → G → A → B → D

Then come back down.

LH: — | RH: D → B → A → G → E

Now try a small musical phrase:

LH: — | RH: E → G → B

Pause for two seconds.

LH: — | RH: D → B → G → E

Play this three or four times. Make the last E softer than the first note. Hear how it creates a feeling of coming home.

2. Four shapes — 6 minutes
1
LH: E + B | RH: G + B + E

Dark, thoughtful

2
LH: C + G | RH: G + C + E

Warmer, opening up

3
LH: G + D | RH: G + B + D

Bright, hopeful

4
LH: D + A | RH: F♯ + A + D

Suspended, wanting to return

Play 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → repeat.

For the first few minutes, press all five notes together on count 1, hold through 2, 3, 4, and release just before the next 1.

Notice the right hand hardly has to move between shapes. That's what makes this progression feel connected.

3. Make the notes roll upward — 5 minutes
Now we're going to turn those block chords into a gentle, rising piano pattern.

Instead of pressing all three right-hand notes together, play them one at a time from lowest to highest.

Count slowly: 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &

1

&

2

&

3

&

4

&

The three highlighted beats are where you press new notes.

For shape 1:

LH: E + B | RH: G → B → E

1: Press LH E + B and RH G.

2: Release RH G and press B.

3: Release RH B and press E.

4: Hold RH E.

Final &: Release everything, ready for the next shape.

Your LH stays down for the entire measure.

Now repeat the same motion for all four shapes:

① LH: E + B | RH: G → B → E

② LH: C + G | RH: G → C → E

③ LH: G + D | RH: G → B → D

④ LH: D + A | RH: F♯ → A → D

This is called a broken chord: the notes that make up a chord are played separately instead of simultaneously.

Play softly at first. Let each rising note feel like it's leading toward something.

First 4 minutes: Repeat your rising-note pattern exactly as written. Keep one shape per 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &. Try to make every chord change land with the beginning of the next drum measure.

Next 4 minutes: Keep holding the LH notes, but occasionally replace the RH rising pattern with a little melody using just:

E – G – A – B – D

For example:

LH: E + B | RH: B → A → G → E

Then play the normal second shape:

LH: C + G | RH: G → C → E

Don't try to write a whole song. Find one short melody you like and repeat it. Let the drums guide how long you leave between notes.

5. Optional GO:KEYS 3 sound — 3 minutes
Try a soft piano layered with a cinematic background sound.

Dual mode means each key plays two instrument sounds together.

Use these two verified factory tones:

UPPER
Concert Piano — Ac.Piano

LOWER
Soft Warm Pad — Synth Pad/Str

Press [split/dual] until you see Dual. Select UPPER with [f2] and choose Concert Piano; select LOWER with [f1] and choose Soft Warm Pad. In Dual mode these are overlapping sound layers, not separate halves of the keyboard.

If the pad overwhelms the piano, open [f4] SETTING for the lower layer and reduce its Level. These are available GO:KEYS 3 tones and controls.

The thing to listen for this week is how repetition, softness, and small changes in volume can make a very simple progression feel dramatic. You don't need more chords. See how much emotion you can get out of just these four.
