🎹 Week 7 — Brazilian-Inspired Pop
A little bossa nova, a little jazz, and a relaxed summer-evening groove
Time: 25 minutes | Tempo: 80 BPM | Difficulty: Easy–Intermediate

This week we're changing direction. Instead of the driving rhythms of disco or electronic music, we're going for something lighter, almost like a conversation between your hands.

Bossa nova is a Brazilian musical style with a gentle, swaying rhythm. The interesting part is that the low notes and higher chords don't always land together.

Today's goal: Learn four smooth hand shapes, then make them dance around each other.

① Warm-up — 3 minutes
Five notes, one hand, no pressure.

Start somewhere around the middle of your keyboard.

LH: — | RH: C → D → E → G → A

Come back down:

LH: — | RH: A → G → E → D → C

Now play this little melody:

LH: — | RH: E → G → A → G → E

Pause.

LH: — | RH: D → E → C

Play these slowly. Don't count yet.

After a minute, experiment with those five notes. Make up little phrases and repeat anything you like.

Listen for: Which notes sound like they're asking a question? Which ones sound like they've finished a sentence?

② Four chords — 6 minutes
We're using four slightly richer sounds than we've played in some previous weeks. A couple of extra notes give them that warm, sophisticated feel.

Play them in this exact order.

①
LH: A + E | RH: G + C + E

②
LH: D + A | RH: F + A + C

③
LH: G + D | RH: F + B + D

④
LH: C + G | RH: E + G + B

Repeat: ① → ② → ③ → ④

For now, forget rhythm.

Press both hands together on 1.

Count 1 – 2 – 3 – 4, holding everything down.

Release just before the next 1, then move to the next shape.

Notice how the right-hand notes don't move very far between chords. That's intentional.

Spend the full six minutes just getting these four shapes comfortable.

③ The rhythm — 5 minutes
Here's where things get interesting.

Previously, we've played chords directly on the numbered beats. Today your right hand will occasionally play between them.

That's called syncopation: emphasizing a moment between the main beats.

Count out loud
1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &

Think of the numbers as your footsteps and the & as the moments between them.

Here's the pattern:

Count	1	&	2	&	3	&	4	&
LH	●	—	—	—	●	—	—	—
RH	—	—	—	●	—	—	●	—
● = Press the notes.

For chord ①:

LH: A + E | RH: G + C + E

Follow these exact instructions:

1: Press LH. Release on the following &.
& after 2: Press RH. Release on 3.
3: Press LH again. Release on the following &.
4: Press RH again. Release on the following &.
Then move to chord ② and repeat.

Every note should be fairly short, but don't make it aggressive.

You're going for:

BOOM ... cha ... BOOM ... cha

Except that first little cha arrives slightly later than you might expect.

Once this clicks, you'll hear that relaxed Brazilian-inspired sway.

④ Over a drum track — 8 minutes
Let's actually make music.

Try this 80 BPM drum-only backing track on YouTube. It's a general groove rather than a dedicated bossa rhythm, but it's slow enough to work with today's pattern. 

First 3 minutes: Just play
Keep cycling through:

① LH: A + E | RH: G + C + E
② LH: D + A | RH: F + A + C
③ LH: G + D | RH: F + B + D
④ LH: C + G | RH: E + G + B

One full count of 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & for each chord.

Don't stop if you miss something. Get back into the groove.

Next 5 minutes: Make it yours
Keep the same four-chord loop, but occasionally replace a right-hand chord with a little melody.

Use only:

C – D – E – G – A

Here's something to try:

LH: A + E | RH: E → G → A

Then return to your usual chord playing.

LH: D + A | RH: F + A + C

Play a little more.

LH: G + D | RH: D → E → G

Then:

LH: C + G | RH: E + G + B

Don't worry about fitting every melody note perfectly into the rhythm yet.

Play a phrase, listen to the drums, and answer yourself.

Try to find one short melody that you genuinely enjoy hearing repeated.

One optional variation
Because you're now playing a bass instrument with your left hand, let's simplify those notes.

Play just one low note instead of two:

① LH: A | RH: G + C + E
② LH: D | RH: F + A + C
③ LH: G | RH: F + B + D
④ LH: C | RH: E + G + B

Use exactly the same rhythm you practiced earlier.

Now you've got a bass player and a guitarist playing together.

⭐ Today's takeaway
We've been building different rhythmic habits over the last several practices.

With disco, you learned to hit the right-hand chords between the beats.

With country, you learned to alternate left and right hands.

With electronic music, you practiced repeating individual notes while holding the harmony underneath.

Today, we're combining some of those skills into a more relaxed, independent rhythm.

The important thing isn't memorizing four more chord names. It's discovering how much the feeling of a piece changes simply by choosing when each hand plays.

And if you find yourself enjoying a particular melody during the last eight minutes, stay with it. That's the part worth developing.
