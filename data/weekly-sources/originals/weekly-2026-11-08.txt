🎹 Week 11 — 80s Synth-Pop
25 minutes · 90 BPM · Roland GO:KEYS 3

This week we're going for the warm, nostalgic keyboard sound of 1980s pop — think Cyndi Lauper and Tears for Fears.

Last week you practiced letting notes rise slowly over a sustained left hand. Today we'll keep that steady left hand, but give the right hand a little rhythmic bounce.

Today's goal: Four easy shapes, one repeating rhythm, and a few minutes making your own little synth-pop song.

1. Warm-up — 3 minutes
Start near the middle of your keyboard, using only your right hand.

LH: — | RH: C → D → E → G → A

Come back down:

LH: — | RH: A → G → E → D → C

Now play this little phrase:

LH: — | RH: E → G → A → G

Pause, then:

LH: — | RH: E → D → C

Play it slowly a few times. Then try making the first note louder and the last note softer. Listen to how even that tiny change makes the phrase feel more expressive.

2. Your four chord shapes — 6 minutes
Keep your left hand below middle C and your right hand near middle C.

1
LH: C + G | RH: G + C + E

Bright, settled

2
LH: G + D | RH: G + B + D

A little anticipation

3
LH: A + E | RH: A + C + E

More emotional

4
LH: F + C | RH: A + C + F

Warm, ready to start again

Play 1 → 2 → 3 → 4, then return to 1.

For now, press both hands together on 1, hold through 2, 3, 4, and release just before the next 1.

Don't rush the transitions. Keep going until your fingers start finding the next shape without having to search for every key.

The little hit between beats 2 and 3 is called an offbeat: a note played halfway between the main numbered beats.

Don't hit the chords aggressively. Aim for a gentle pulse with a little spring to it.

4. Play over a drumbeat — 8 minutes
90 BPM — Simple Straight Beat

Lumbeat · Drum-only backing track

A steady, uncomplicated beat with no competing keyboard harmony. 

Jamtrack 964

First 4 minutes: Play the same four shapes, using the exact rhythm above.

① LH: C + G | RH: G + C + E

② LH: G + D | RH: G + B + D

③ LH: A + E | RH: A + C + E

④ LH: F + C | RH: A + C + F

One full 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & for each shape. Let the drummer keep you in time.

Next 4 minutes — one optional variation: Keep the first two right-hand chord hits exactly the same. But on beat 4, play only one higher note instead of the full chord.

For that last hit, use:

① LH: C + G (held) | RH: E

② LH: G + D (held) | RH: D

③ LH: A + E (held) | RH: E

④ LH: F + C (held) | RH: F

This gives you a tiny melody on top of the repeating chords.

Once that sounds comfortable, try choosing your own note for beat 4. Don't change anything else.

Your quick keyboard cheat sheet
Four shapes. One rhythm.
① LH: C + G | RH: G + C + E

② LH: G + D | RH: G + B + D

③ LH: A + E | RH: A + C + E

④ LH: F + C | RH: A + C + F

LH: Press on 1, hold all the way through 4, release on the final &.

RH: Press on 1, the & after 2, and 4. Release between hits.

This week's skill is making a repeating chord pattern feel alive. You're combining the held notes from last week's cinematic session with the short rhythmic hits you've practiced in disco and funk. The real progress happens when the rhythm starts feeling automatic and you're listening to the sound instead of concentrating on which finger goes where.
