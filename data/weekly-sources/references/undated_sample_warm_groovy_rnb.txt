🎹 Sample Week — Warm, Groovy R&B
Time: ~25 minutes
Goal: Get one 4-chord groove comfortable enough that you stop thinking about the chords and start making music with them.
1. First, just find the sound — 3 minutes
Don't worry about chords yet.

With your right hand, play only these black keys:

D♭ • E♭ • G♭ • A♭ • B♭

Start anywhere. Play slowly.

Try this little phrase:

A♭ → B♭ → G♭ → E♭

Then answer it:

G♭ → A♭ → E♭ → D♭

Don't worry about counting. Play them like you're singing two sentences.

Once your fingers start wandering and you accidentally play something you like, repeat it. That's more important than my phrase.
2. Your four chords — 7 minutes
We're going to make these easy on your hands.
① D♭
LH: D♭ + A♭  |  RH: D♭ + F + A♭

⬇️
② B♭ minor
LH: B♭ + F  |  RH: D♭ + F + B♭

⬇️
③ G♭
LH: G♭ + D♭  |  RH: D♭ + G♭ + B♭

⬇️
④ A♭
LH: A♭ + E♭  |  RH: C + E♭ + A♭

⬇️ back to ①

D♭ → B♭m → G♭ → A♭

For now, hit every chord with both hands together and hold it.

Count:

1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &

Change to the next chord on the next 1.

Don't add rhythm yet. Get to where you can cycle through all four without stopping to figure out where your fingers go.

And notice something useful: your RH doesn't actually travel very far between these chords.
3. Now give it a groove — 5 minutes
Once the changes feel okay, let's make it sound less like chord practice.

Count:

1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &

Do this:

1: BOTH hands hit the chord
2: nothing — let it ring
3: RIGHT hand hits the chord again
4: nothing

So:

BOTH — — — RH — — —

Then change chords and repeat.

Don't speed this up yet.

Once you feel yourself starting to bounce a little instead of count, you're getting it.
4. Now the fun part — 7 minutes
Find a beat-only track around 75–85 BPM.

Keep this going:

D♭ → B♭m → G♭ → A♭

But now simplify your LH.

Instead of two notes, play only:

LH: D♭ → B♭ → G♭ → A♭

One bass note at the beginning of each chord.

Your RH keeps playing the chords.

After a couple of loops, stop playing RH chords entirely.

Remember those black keys from the beginning?

D♭ • E♭ • G♭ • A♭ • B♭

Start playing with them over your bass notes.

Don't try to “write a melody.”

Play something.

Pause.

Answer yourself.

Repeat something you liked.

If you hit something and think “oooooh” — play it again. 😆

That's the whole point.
5. OPTIONAL: Make the GO:KEYS sound gorgeous — 3 minutes
Don't even touch this part until the progression is comfortable.

For the Roland GO:KEYS 3, I'd start simple:

Main/Right Hand: Electric Piano
Left Hand: warm Synth Pad

Keep the pad low in volume compared with the electric piano.

Then:

LH: D♭ → B♭ → G♭ → A♭
RH: improvise using those five black keys.

The left side creates this big warm bed while your right hand gets to float around on top.

You don't need to fill the space. Let notes hang.
🎧 What we're aiming toward
For the feeling rather than the literal chords:

Damage — H.E.R.

Listen to how relaxed the keyboard sits inside the groove. Nothing is rushing to the next chord. There's space, and the rhythm section does a lot of the work.

Also try Best Part by Daniel Caesar featuring H.E.R.. Listen specifically to how a small repeating harmonic idea can carry an entire mood.
⭐ What counts as success today
Not memorizing four chord names.

Not understanding why they're theoretically related.

It's this:
You turn on a beat, your hands automatically find D♭ → B♭m → G♭ → A♭, and eventually you start screwing around with it because you're bored of playing it correctly.

That's when I want you composing.

And this is much closer to how I'd structure your Monday lessons going forward.
