Weekly Keyboard Practice (20–30 minutes)
Theme: Finding an idea and letting it evolve
The goal this week isn't to learn a song—it's to discover a short musical phrase that could become your piece.

1. Black-Key Exploration (5 minutes)
Set a simple metronome or beat-only track around 70–80 BPM.

Using only the black keys, spend a few minutes improvising without worrying about harmony. Since the black keys form a pentatonic scale, almost everything will sound cohesive.

Try:

Short 3–5 note ideas.
Lots of silence between phrases.
Repeating one motif, changing only the rhythm.
Don't chase complexity—look for one phrase you genuinely like.

2. New Chord Voicings (7 minutes)
Keep your right hand improvising on the black keys while your left hand experiments with these simple voicings:

B♭sus2 → (B♭–C–F)
G♭maj7(no3) → (G♭–D♭–F)
D♭add9 → (D♭–E♭–A♭)
Don't think about the names while playing. Listen for:

Which chord feels like "home."
Which chord creates tension.
Which transition surprises you.
Hold each chord for several measures before changing.

3. Split Voice Experiment (5–7 minutes)
On your Roland GO:KEYS 3:

Left: Warm pad or soft strings.
Right: Felt piano or mellow electric piano.
Play sustained chords with the pad while letting the piano speak in short melodic phrases.

Focus on dynamics:

Let the pad stay almost motionless.
Allow the piano to become the storyteller.
4. Build Your Own Piece (8–10 minutes)
Keep the beat-only track running.

Challenge yourself to create:

An A section (about 20–30 seconds).
Repeat it.
Change only one thing:
rhythm,
ending,
bass note,
or one chord.
That's the beginning of a B section.

Record the whole improvisation on your keyboard or phone. Don't stop if you make mistakes—sometimes the best ideas happen when you're trying to recover.

Weekly Keyboard Practice (20–30 minutes)
Theme: Movement Through Suspended Harmony
This week, focus on creating music that feels like it's always moving forward without needing a dramatic melody. Think shimmering, suspended, and patient.

1. Black-Key Exploration (5 minutes)
Set a beat-only track between 78–88 BPM.

Stay entirely on the black keys. Instead of running up and down the scale, limit yourself to four notes for the first few minutes.

Try this idea:

Play a short motif.
Repeat it three times.
On the fourth repetition, change only one note.
Amazing ideas often come from tiny changes rather than brand-new phrases.

2. Chord Voicing Exploration (7 minutes)
This week, experiment with open voicings that leave lots of space.

Try these left-hand shapes:

D♭maj9 (no 3rd) → D♭ – A♭ – E♭
A♭add9 → A♭ – E♭ – B♭
G♭maj7 → G♭ – D♭ – F
Hold each chord while your right hand improvises only on the black keys.

Notice how changing the bass note underneath the same melody completely changes the emotional color.

3. Split/Dual Voice Experiment (6 minutes)
On your Roland GO:KEYS 3:

Left hand: Warm analog pad or soft synth strings.
Right hand: Electric piano or a mellow bell/felt piano layer.
Keep the pad sustaining underneath while your right hand plays lightly. Aim for contrast: broad, slow harmony below and delicate movement above.

If your keyboard supports it, add just a touch of reverb to let notes bloom into each other.

4. Build Your Own Piece (10 minutes)
Continue over the beat-only track.

Structure your improvisation like this:

30 seconds: Introduce one motif.
30 seconds: Repeat it with a different chord underneath.
30 seconds: Strip it back to almost nothing.
30 seconds: Bring the full texture back with both hands.
Record the session. Afterwards, listen back once and identify one moment that made you stop and think, "I'd like to hear that again." That's your starting point for your next original piece.

Weekly Keyboard Practice (20–30 minutes)

For the first five minutes, stay entirely on the black keys.

Rules:

Play only 2–4 notes at a time.
Leave at least one measure of silence after every phrase.
Repeat each idea before changing it.
Listen for which motifs naturally invite a chord underneath.

2. Chord Voicing Study (7 minutes)
This week, explore quartal-inspired (stacked fourths) and suspended sounds.

Try these left-hand shapes:

E♭ – A♭ – D♭
B♭ – E♭ – A♭
D♭ – G♭ – B♭
Don't worry about naming every chord. Instead, notice how they create openness and avoid sounding overly "major" or "minor."

While holding each voicing, improvise gently on the black keys with your right hand.

3. Split/Dual Voice Exercise (6 minutes)
On your Roland GO:KEYS 3:

Left hand: Warm synth pad or soft strings.
Right hand: Felt piano layered lightly with a bell or airy synth.
Try this approach:

Sustain a chord for four measures.
Play a simple melody.
Instead of changing the melody, change only the underlying chord.
Observe how the emotional character shifts even though the notes stay similar.

0:00–0:20 — Introduce a motif.

0:40–1:00 — Strip it back to almost nothing, then end with a sustained chord.
Record the entire take. When you listen back, don't evaluate the performance—identify the single phrase that has the strongest emotional pull. That's the idea worth developing next week.

Weekly Keyboard Practice (20–30 minutes)
Theme: Tension, Release, and Long Lines
This week is about making music that feels like it's going somewhere. Instead of thinking in individual notes, think in phrases that slowly unfold over several measures.

1. Black-Key Exploration (5 minutes)
Choose a beat-only track around 72–84 BPM.

Stay entirely on the black keys.

For the first few minutes, use this rule:

Play one short phrase (3–6 notes).
Wait two beats.
Answer it with a different phrase.
Repeat that "conversation" several times before inventing something new.
Imagine you're writing a melody that someone could hum.

2. Chord Voicing Discovery (7 minutes)
This week, explore major 9 and add9 colors that feel spacious without sounding overly jazzy.

Try these left-hand voicings:

G♭maj9 (open) → G♭ – D♭ – F – A♭
D♭add9 → D♭ – A♭ – E♭
A♭6/9 → A♭ – E♭ – F – B♭
Don't worry about perfect theory. Listen for how each chord changes the emotional weight of the exact same melody.

As an experiment, keep your right hand playing the same melodic idea while changing only the left-hand chord.

3. Split/Dual Voice Texture (6 minutes)
On your Roland GO:KEYS 3:

Left hand: Soft analog pad or warm strings.
Right hand: Felt piano layered quietly with a glassy synth or soft EP.
Begin with just the pad for four measures.

Then fade the piano in naturally.

Rather than playing louder, make the arrangement feel bigger simply by adding another layer.

4. Build an Original Sketch (8–10 minutes)
Keep the beat running.

Create a simple two-part structure:

Part A (30 seconds)

Sparse melody.
One or two chords.
Lots of breathing room.
Part B (30 seconds)

Same motif.
New bass movement.
Slightly fuller texture.
Then return to Part A, but play it more quietly than when you started.

Record the entire minute and don't stop for mistakes. You're collecting ideas, not performing.
