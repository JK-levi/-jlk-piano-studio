## This week: **80s synth-pop / open-road feeling**

**Time:** about 25 minutes  
**Goal:** get one bright, slightly nostalgic 4-chord loop comfortable enough that you can play it without thinking, then start adding your own melody over it.

### 1. Black-key warm-up — 4 minutes

Use only:

**RH: D♭ – E♭ – G♭ – A♭ – B♭**

Start with this simple shape:

**RH: D♭ → E♭ → G♭ → E♭**

Pause.

Then answer it with:

**RH: A♭ → G♭ → E♭ → D♭**

Play it slowly a few times.

Then keep the same notes but change the rhythm however you want. The point is to get your ear involved before the chord loop starts.

---

## 2. Main 4-chord loop — 7 minutes

Play these in order:

**①** LH: **D♭ + A♭** | RH: **F + A♭ + D♭**

**②** LH: **A♭ + E♭** | RH: **E♭ + A♭ + C**

**③** LH: **B♭ + F** | RH: **D♭ + F + B♭**

**④** LH: **G♭ + D♭** | RH: **D♭ + G♭ + B♭**

Then go back to **①**.

For now, use no fancy rhythm.

Count:

**1 – 2 – 3 – 4**

On **1**: press both hands.  
Hold through **2, 3, 4**.  
Release just before the next **1**.

Keep looping:

**D♭ → A♭ → B♭ → G♭**

You do not need to memorize those names. Just learn the four hand shapes and the order.

---

## 3. Give it the 80s pulse — 5 minutes

Now count:

**1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &**

For each chord:

**1:** press BOTH hands  
**& after 2:** release and replay RH only  
**3:** keep holding  
**4:** keep holding

So the feel is:

**BOTH — — — — RH — — —**

That second RH hit arriving a little early gives you a more driving pop feel.

Do this on every chord.

### One optional variation

Once that feels easy, play the RH again on the **& after 4** too.

That means the right hand gives you a little push into the next chord.

---

## 4. Beat-only section — 7–8 minutes

Find a beat-only track around **90–100 BPM**.

Start with the full loop:

**LH: D♭ + A♭ | RH: F + A♭ + D♭**  
**LH: A♭ + E♭ | RH: E♭ + A♭ + C**  
**LH: B♭ + F | RH: D♭ + F + B♭**  
**LH: G♭ + D♭ | RH: D♭ + G♭ + B♭**

Play several loops until the rhythm feels automatic.

Then simplify the left hand:

**LH: D♭ → A♭ → B♭ → G♭**

Now let your RH stop playing full chords and go back to:

**D♭ – E♭ – G♭ – A♭ – B♭**

Try this rule:

**Play 4–6 notes → stop for a full bar → answer yourself.**

You are not trying to “solo.” You are trying to find one little melody that feels like it belongs over the loop.

If you land on something good, repeat it instead of immediately changing it.
