## This week: **One melody, changing ground**

This week I want you to get away from “new melody = new section.” Instead, keep **one recognizable melodic idea** and make it feel different by changing what happens *underneath* it. That’s a very useful composition trick for the atmospheric/electronic direction you’ve been exploring.

**0:00–5:00 — Black keys, but with a bass anchor.** Put on a beat-only track around **84–92 BPM**. Right hand: black keys only. Left hand: alternate just **G♭ and E♭**, low on the keyboard, holding each for 2–4 bars. Find a little 4–6 note phrase you like and deliberately repeat it. Notice how the exact same black-key melody feels different over G♭ versus E♭.

**5:00–12:00 — Turn those bass notes into harmony.** Try this progression slowly:

**G♭maj9** → G♭ – D♭ – F – A♭  
**E♭m9** → E♭ – B♭ – D♭ – F  
**B♭m7** → B♭ – F – A♭ – D♭  
**D♭sus2/add6** → D♭ – A♭ – B♭ – E♭

The nice thing here is that your right hand can continue living almost entirely on the **black keys**, while the left hand supplies the notes that define the harmony. Play your original motif over all four chords rather than inventing something new for each one.

Then start breaking the chords apart. Instead of *G♭–D♭–F–A♭* simultaneously, try **G♭ → D♭ → A♭ → F** as a slow repeating pattern. Suddenly you've got the beginning of an accompaniment rather than just chord practice.

**12:00–18:00 — Split voices.** Try **mellow/felt piano on the right** and a **warm pad on the left**. Don't let the pad play full four-note chords; that's likely to get muddy. Give it only the root + fifth — G♭/D♭, E♭/B♭, etc. Let the piano provide all the interesting extensions above it.

Experiment with register too. Move your melody **an octave higher than feels natural**. That separation between low pad and high, intimate piano can make a very simple idea sound enormous.

**18:00–27:00 — Make it into something.** Restart your beat and record one uninterrupted pass. Begin with only the G♭ bass and your motif. Introduce the full progression. Add the pad. Then, about halfway through, **remove the beat mentally from your playing**: hold notes across the bar lines rather than hitting everything neatly on beats 1 and 3.

For the ending, return to G♭maj9—but **don't resolve the A♭ down to G♭**. Leave that 9th hanging. See whether you prefer the slightly unresolved feeling.
