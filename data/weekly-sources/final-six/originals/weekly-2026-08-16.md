## This week: **Make the rhythm part of the composition**

We’ve spent several weeks making harmony and texture do the heavy lifting. This time, keep that atmospheric quality but make your **keyboard itself participate in the groove**. Think less “piano floating over a beat” and more “piano is one of the percussion instruments.”

**0:00–5:00 — Black-key rhythm game.** Put on a beat-only track around **92–100 BPM**. Pick only three black keys with your right hand—try **G♭, A♭, B♭**. For the first minute, play just one note and find a rhythm that locks with the beat. Then gradually introduce the other two notes without changing that rhythmic identity.

The important bit: **don't play on every obvious beat.** Try entering just after the kick or leaving beat 1 completely empty. You’re looking for something that makes your head move before it makes you think “nice melody.”

**5:00–12:00 — New chords, tighter shapes.** Here’s your progression:

**E♭m9** → E♭ – G♭ – B♭ – D♭ – F  
**B♭m7** → B♭ – D♭ – F – A♭  
**G♭maj9** → G♭ – B♭ – D♭ – F – A♭  
**A♭sus4 → A♭** → A♭ – D♭ – E♭ → A♭ – C – E♭

But don't try grabbing all five notes with one hand. Start with a low **root or root + fifth in your left hand**, then put just the upper 2–3 notes in your right.

For example, on E♭m9:

**LH:** E♭ – B♭  
**RH:** D♭ – F – G♭

That's the sound I want you to notice this week. It's much richer than a basic E♭ minor, but your hands aren't doing anything particularly difficult.

**12:00–18:00 — Make those chords groove.** Instead of holding each chord for four beats, try:

**HIT — rest — HIT ——— rest**

Then move the second hit around. Put it slightly earlier. Put it later. Skip it entirely once.

This is where you're composing. A chord progression gives you harmony; **where you choose to place those chords gives the piece personality.**

For split voices, try **electric piano on the right + a rounded synth bass on the left** this week rather than another pad. Keep the bass extremely simple—roots are enough. If that starts sounding like you're playing in a hotel lobby, simplify the EP part and make it more rhythmic.

**18:00–27:00 — Your piece.** Take whichever little rhythmic figure you found in the first five minutes and bring it back over the full progression. Don't write a new melody yet.

Do three passes:

**Pass 1:** bass + rhythmic chords only.  
**Pass 2:** add your three-note black-key motif.  
**Pass 3:** start sparse, build into everything, then abruptly remove everything except the motif.

Record Pass 3.

And one slightly weird experiment: on the final time through the progression, **keep your right-hand pattern identical but stop playing the bass notes.** You'll hear how much your ear has been using the bass to interpret what those upper notes “mean.”
