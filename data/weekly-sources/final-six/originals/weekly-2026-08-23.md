## This week: **Make the bass change the meaning**

Last week was rhythmic; this week I’d go back toward harmony—but with a trick that’s especially useful when you’re writing your own stuff: **keep the upper chord nearly identical while moving the bass underneath it.** You get dramatic harmonic movement without having to think up four completely different chords.

### 0:00–5:00 — Black keys, one little shape

Put on a beat-only track around **86–94 BPM**.

Right hand only. Make this tiny shape your home base:

**G♭ – A♭ – D♭**

Play those notes together, broken apart, backwards, repeated rhythmically—whatever starts feeling musical. Then freely add **E♭ and B♭**, but stay on black keys.

The goal isn't a scale exercise. Find **one rhythm/phrase that sounds like something *you'd actually use*.**

### 5:00–12:00 — Here's the fun part

Keep your RH playing:

**G♭ – A♭ – D♭**

Now move single bass notes underneath it:

**D♭ → E♭ → B♭ → G♭**

Listen to what happens.

Then expand those into these voicings:

**D♭sus4**  
LH: D♭ – A♭  
RH: G♭ – A♭ – D♭

**E♭m11**  
LH: E♭ – B♭  
RH: G♭ – A♭ – D♭

**B♭m7(add♭7/11 color)**  
LH: B♭ – F  
RH: G♭ – A♭ – D♭

**G♭add9**  
LH: G♭ – D♭  
RH: G♭ – A♭ – D♭

Don't get hung up on the chord labels. **The important discovery is that your right hand barely moved.**

Play the progression:

**D♭ → E♭m → B♭m → G♭**

Four beats each at first. Then try **2 / 2 / 4 / 8 beats** and hear how much more intentional it suddenly sounds.

### 12:00–18:00 — Dual voice: separate the jobs

Try something different from our usual piano + pad setup:

**Voice 1:** warm electric piano  
**Voice 2:** very soft synth/strings

If you can adjust their balance, make the synth considerably quieter.

Play the RH voicing on the layered sound, but let your **left hand play only bass notes**.

Then deliberately lift your RH while the bass keeps moving.

You'll hear something useful: sometimes the *absence* of the chord creates more tension than changing to another chord.

Bring the RH back on G♭ and it'll feel much larger than it actually is.

### 18:00–27:00 — Turn it into your own thing

Start the beat again and record.

Don't predetermine a melody. Instead:

**First pass:** bass + your three-note RH shape.

**Second pass:** start breaking G♭–A♭–D♭ into a rhythmic pattern.

**Third pass:** let one of those broken patterns accidentally become a melody. Follow it.

Then here's your challenge: when you reach the **B♭ bass**, introduce **E♭** into your RH melody.

That one extra black key gives you a gorgeous B♭m11-ish color.

If your ear goes, *oooh, THAT*—stop thinking about the exercise. Stay there and explore it. That's exactly what we're looking for.
